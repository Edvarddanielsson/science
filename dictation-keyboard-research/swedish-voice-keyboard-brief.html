Swedish Voice Keyboard Brief

Which iPhone dictation keyboards reviewers rate highest, which ones have features worth copying, why people stick with them, and whether a Swedish version can run entirely on the phone with Klang Pianissimo for speech and Gemma 4 E2B for cleanup.

At a glance

  1. 1

    Wispr Flow is the product to beat. It holds 4.8 stars from about 17,000 US App Store ratings, far more than any rival, and 9to5Mac and TechCrunch rate it highly. Among the rivals, Aqua Voice and Willow got the strongest individual reviews.123422

  2. 2

    The habit comes from not having to edit. Wispr tracks how often people send dictated text without changing it. It reports about 5–10% for Apple and Google dictation and 85–89% for Flow. The median time from seeing the text to pressing send is half a second. These are company figures.2728

  3. 3

    iOS keeps getting in the way. Keyboard extensions can't record audio, so every app sends you to its main app to start a session. Since iOS 26.4 that app can no longer send you back automatically, and it can't tell which app you came from.6713

  4. 4

    Swedish is underserved. Apple's Swedish dictation runs on the device but has no automatic punctuation. Apple's new LLM-based Advanced Dictation in iOS 27 is English-only and needs an iPhone with 12 GB of RAM. I found no Swedish-first AI keyboard.119

  5. 5

    Fully local keyboards already exist, from small developers. Outspoken, KeySay and Yaps run speech recognition and a cleanup pass on the phone, and Outspoken and KeySay use an on-device LLM for it. None of them targets Swedish.161718

  6. 6

    Your stack can work behind a keyboard, with limits. The models have to run in the main app while it is in the background. There the GPU is off limits, and from iOS 27 the Neural Engine needs a new Background Inference entitlement. Gemma 4 E2B on the CPU decodes about 25 tokens per second on an iPhone 17 Pro, so stream the text into the field or use a smaller cleanup model.1237

Background

How a voice keyboard works on iPhone

iOS doesn't let a keyboard extension use the microphone, even with Full Access and microphone permission granted. Developers who try get audio-session errors.13 Every app in this report therefore has two parts: a thin keyboard and the main app (Apple calls it the containing app), which holds the microphone and does the work.1517

What the user goes through

  1. They tap the mic in your keyboard.
  2. If no session is running, the keyboard opens your app, which starts recording. 9to5Mac calls this "one unavoidably clunky step imposed by Apple's security."3
  3. Since iOS 26.4 the app can't return them automatically. They swipe right along the bottom edge to get back. Wispr Flow and Monologue both document this.624
  4. The session stays alive in the background. Wispr lets users end it after 5 minutes, 15 minutes, an hour, or never, so later dictations start straight from the keyboard.2
  5. Your app sends the finished text back, and the keyboard types it where the cursor is.

What iOS 26.4 changed

A developer report says the containing app can no longer find out which app the user came from, so it can't reopen it.7 Wispr Flow's help page confirms both effects: you may have to swipe back by hand, and "your writing style no longer changes between apps." Wispr now uses one default style, which you can override from the keyboard.6 TechCrunch describes it as Apple "restricting some of these apps."8

What iOS 27 added

Apple built LLM-based dictation into its own keyboard. It runs on an on-device model called AFM Core Advanced and adds punctuation and capitalisation as you speak. It needs 12 GB of RAM, which in the beta meant only the iPhone 17 Pro, 17 Pro Max and iPhone Air, and it supports English only.91011 Tom's Guide found it faster and more accurate than iOS 26 dictation, with inconsistent punctuation in the first beta.10 On Android, Google's Gboard has a similar Gemini-based feature called Rambler.33

List 2

The most interesting features, in my judgment

These are picked for what they do, not for how popular they are. Each card ends with the idea I would take from it.

Aqua Voice

Cloud

An edit mode lets you change the last transcript or a selection by voice: "remove filler words, this is a Slack message," "translate this to Japanese," "spell Toni, T-O-N-I." The vendor says text starts appearing in under 50 ms and is final in about 450 ms.20

TakeEditing by voice. On a phone, moving the cursor to fix one word is the slowest step there is.

Outspoken

On device

A small indie keyboard that transcribes on the phone with Apple's engine, Parakeet v3 or Whisper Large v3 Turbo, then rewrites or translates with Apple Intelligence or Qwen3, also on the phone. Early App Store reviews report model downloads failing.16

TakeProof that an on-device LLM pass behind a keyboard ships today. Letting users pick the engine.

KeySay

On device

On-device Whisper, plus Apple's on-device model to fix misheard words and remove filler words. A "your own fixes" list maps words it tends to mishear to what you meant. You pay once instead of subscribing.17

TakeCorrection pairs taught by the user. Pay-once pricing, which only works because inference costs nothing per word.

Yaps

On device

Speech recognition, cleanup and read-aloud all run on the phone. A guest mode works without an account. Dictations land in a local notes vault, and audio files can be transcribed too.18

TakeOnboarding with no account. Read-aloud as a way to proofread.

Typeless

Cloud

Notices when you change your mind mid-sentence and keeps only the final version, removes repeated words, turns spoken lists into lists, and adapts tone to the app. "Speak to edit" revises text you already have.21

TakeHandling self-corrections. Swedish speakers do it constantly: "nej, förresten…"

Willow

Cloud

A full QWERTY keyboard under the mic, one-tap rewrites (fix grammar, formal, friendly) and presets for work and casual messages. A session timeout and a lock-screen widget let you switch the microphone off.4

TakeSession controls that make an always-on microphone feel safe.

Superwhisper

Mixed

User-defined Modes, each a custom prompt, that you can switch from the keyboard. TechCrunch notes you can see both the raw and the processed transcript. Bring your own API keys.119

TakeModes. They are how power users make the tool their own.

Wispr Flow

Cloud

Whisper mode for dictating quietly, Snippets, quick notes, 20-minute dictations, and a style you can override from the keyboard. An optional QWERTY layout sits behind the default number pad.224142

TakeRecognition that still works when you whisper.

Monologue

Cloud

Starts from the Action Button, Control Center or Apple Watch, with a Live Activity on the lock screen. Many quick captures never touch the keyboard, so they skip the bounce.24

TakeWays to start that don't go through the keyboard.

Zavi

Cloud

"Voice to action": the keyboard can carry out a spoken command, such as sending an email through your Gmail account. The claims come from the developer only.34

TakeNothing yet. Worth watching.

VocaPhone

On device

An open-source (AGPL-3.0) keyboard plus containing app. It records in the app and runs speech-to-text on the phone or on a server you host yourself. The iPhone version is in public TestFlight.15

TakeReadable reference code for the architecture. Mind the licence before copying any of it.

Gboard Rambler

Mixed

Android only. Google's Gemini dictation removes fillers, keeps only your corrected version ("3 p.m., actually make that 2 p.m."), and handles switching languages mid-sentence.33

TakeThe bar users will expect. Platform owners are now doing cleanup themselves.

Behaviour

Why people get hooked

3×faster text entry by speech than an iPhone keyboard, with 20.4% fewer errors (English)Stanford, Baidu, UW study30
89%of Flow messages sent without edits, up from 45% a year earlierFounder, Dec 202527
0.5 smedian time from seeing the text to pressing sendFounder27
70%of users still active after 12 monthsFounder29
20%of free users convert to paid, against 3–4% typical for freemiumFounder, Jul 202528

Only the first figure is independent research. The rest are Wispr's own, unaudited numbers.

  1. Talking is about three times faster than thumb-typing.

    The study measured it on a phone keyboard, where typing is slowest. Wispr's founder says users average about 120 words per minute with Flow, against roughly 40 for typing.3043

  2. Trust removes proofreading.

    Once the output is right nearly every time, people stop reading it. Each message sent in half a second is a small win, and the loop repeats dozens of times a day.27

  3. You can think out loud in fragments.

    One long-time user describes it as "bullet points in, full messages out." Typing makes you compose as you go; talking lets you say the whole thought and have it shaped afterwards.32

  4. It spreads from one app to all of them.

    People start with one kind of message and drift into using it everywhere. Wispr reports that across its users, about 75% of characters now come from Flow and 25% from the keyboard.27

  5. It makes AI tools better.

    Spoken prompts are longer and carry more context than typed ones, so answers improve and people dictate even more. Wispr's founder contrasts "toilet broken how fix" with a 30-second spoken explanation.43

Can a local LLM sit behind the keyboard?

Yes, but never inside the keyboard itself. Everything heavy runs in the containing app while it sits in the background. These are the constraints to design around:

ConstraintWhat it means for you
Keyboard extensions can't record audio.13Record in the containing app and pass text through an App Group.
iOS kills a keyboard at roughly 48–77 MB. One developer saw the kill at 77 MB on an iPhone 14 Pro.14No models in the keyboard. It is a remote control and a text inserter.
Starting needs a trip to the app, and since iOS 26.4 there is no automatic return and no way to tell which app the user came from.67Long-lived sessions, a clear swipe-back screen, and a manual style switch.
Background apps can't use the GPU except in a BGContinuedProcessingTask with the GPU entitlement. From iOS 27, any Neural Engine use in the background requires the Background Inference entitlement (Apple's documentation, marked beta).12The CPU is the only path that always works. Apply for the entitlement, and measure Pianissimo on the Neural Engine in the background on iOS 26 and 27.
Background apps are the first to be killed when memory runs low. Pianissimo's int8 Core ML encoder is about 650 MB of weights; Gemma 4 E2B on CPU uses about 607 MB according to Google.3837Load the models in sequence and unload after use, or keep only the speech model warm.
Holding a recording session in the background affects other audio. One user reports music quality dropping and AirPods controls stopping while a dictation app held the mic.16Release the session promptly and make the timeout visible.
Apple's Foundation Models run on the device, need no download, and Apple Intelligence supports Swedish. Apple documents a rate limit that applies only when the app is in the background.1139Test calling it from the keyboard, which is in the foreground. I found nothing confirming whether that works.

A full rewrite on the CPU takes about eight times longer than the cloud leaders. Streaming hides most of that. At 25 tokens per second the text arrives at about 14 words per second, faster than anyone reads, so the user sees words start about 0.6 seconds after they stop talking.

The architecture I'd use for a Swedish keyboard

Keyboard extension

Swedish QWERTY with å, ä and ö, a large mic key and a style switch (Formell, Vardaglig, Kort). It inserts text as it streams in. It holds no models and no audio.

Containing app, in the background

Records, and runs Pianissimo in chunks while the user is still talking, so recognition is nearly done when they stop. A rules pass handles numbers and obvious fillers. The LLM pass streams tokens to the keyboard. Dictionary and history stay encrypted on the phone.

LLM options, in the order I'd test them:

  1. A small model trained only for Swedish dictation cleanup, in the 270M–1B range, distilled from a larger model. A narrow task suits a small model, and a small model is fast on the CPU.
  2. Gemma 4 E2B on the CPU, streamed. It is already in your app and needs no new training.
  3. Apple's Foundation Models, where Apple Intelligence is on. There is nothing to download and it handles Swedish, but check its Swedish quality and the background rate limit. From iOS 27 you can put Gemma and Apple's model behind the same LanguageModelSession interface and swap them.40
  4. Gemma on the GPU, only when your own app is in the foreground, for example for long notes inside Molnfri.

Recommendation

The Swedish build

Positioning: Wispr Flow's polish, in Swedish, with nothing leaving the phone. The gap is real. Apple's Swedish dictation has no punctuation, its new AI dictation is English-only, and the apps reviewers love are US cloud services.11

The asset: Klang reports that Pianissimo averages 4.5% word error rate across five Swedish test sets. KB-Whisper scores 5.8% for small (your current model), 4.9% for medium and 3.5% for large. On Klang's dialect set Pianissimo scores 4.9%, against 5.2% for KB-Whisper small. These are Klang's own numbers. The model is licensed CC BY 4.0.3538

What to build first, in order

  1. Swedish text that needs no editing, covering everything in the table above, measured on your own Swedish test set.
  2. A session that survives iOS 26.4 and later: start once, a long timeout, a Live Activity with a Stop button, starts from the Action Button and Control Center, and a clear swipe-back screen.
  3. A personal dictionary that learns from corrections made in your own keyboard.
  4. A full Swedish keyboard with the style switch.
  5. Voice edits and translation to English.

Later: snippets, quiet dictation, shared team dictionaries for clinics and law firms, Apple Watch capture.

Measure

The share of Swedish dictations sent without edits. Seconds from the end of speech to text on screen. Word error rate, including dialects. The share of dictations that needed a trip to the app. Four-week retention.

Price against

Wispr $15/mo. Willow $15/mo or $143.99/yr. Typeless about $12/mo yearly. Monologue $10/mo. Superwhisper $8.49/mo or $249.99 lifetime. Outspoken €1.99/mo, €17.99/yr or €44.99 lifetime. With no server cost per word, a low price or a lifetime licence is viable.11623

Risks

  • Apple adds Swedish to Advanced Dictation. Its language lists grow every year. Compete on the dictionary, voice editing, Swedish style and professional workflows, not on punctuation alone.
  • Apple restricts keyboards further, as it did in iOS 26.4.
  • Apple doesn't grant the Background Inference entitlement. Then recognition and cleanup both run on the CPU.
  • The Core ML conversion of Pianissimo is a young community build. Reproduce it before you ship.38

Next steps

Prototype gates for the keyboard version

These extend the gates in your Molnfri handoff. Run them on an iPhone 15 Pro with a Release build, on both iOS 26 and iOS 27.38

  1. Session. The keyboard-to-app trip and the swipe back work on iOS 26.4+ and 27, and background recording survives 30 minutes with the screen locked.
  2. Recognition in the background. Pianissimo on the Neural Engine while backgrounded, on iOS 26 and on iOS 27 with and without the entitlement. The CPU real-time factor as the fallback.
  3. Cleanup. Gemma 4 E2B on the CPU in the background: time to first token, tokens per second, and streaming into the keyboard. The same test with Apple's Foundation Models, called from the keyboard and from the app.
  4. Memory. Peak footprint with both models loaded, and whether iOS kills your app when the user opens the Camera.
  5. Quality. The share of 100 real Swedish dictations that need no edits: raw recognition, rules only, Gemma, and Apple's model.
